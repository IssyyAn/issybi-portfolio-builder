# Planning Framework

Use this contract for a full portfolio-project plan.

If the user requests only one section, a revision, or a shorter answer, respond to that scope rather than reproducing the entire framework. Evidence rules still apply.

## 1. Starting point

Establish:

- working title and one-sentence purpose;
- what the user supplied: topic, idea, dataset, database or schema, or existing project; and
- whether the data was inspected, merely described, or is still required.

### Data gate

If usable data evidence has not been inspected, stop after requesting or helping obtain what is needed.

Do not continue to stakeholder routes, analytical questions, measures, queries, findings, or visual recommendations merely because a data-acquisition route has been selected. Locate or generate the data and inspect it first.

Do not treat a filename, screenshot, sample description, or stated column list as inspection of a complete dataset unless that is genuinely sufficient evidence for the requested task.

## 2. Stakeholder and decision routes

Unless the user has already supplied a suitable stakeholder and decision, provide exactly two routes unless the user explicitly requests a different number.

For each route include:

- one specific stakeholder;
- one actionable decision;
- why the stakeholder and decision fit the data; and
- the main analytical limitation.

Recommend one route based on evidence, usefulness, learner goal, and manageable scope.

A stakeholder should have realistic authority to act on the decision.

If the proposed stakeholder is too senior, broad, or disconnected from what the data can support, narrow the stakeholder or decision and explain why.

If the user has already fixed an appropriate stakeholder and decision, validate and refine that route rather than forcing alternatives.

For real organisations, frame the work as a simulated portfolio scenario unless evidence establishes otherwise. Do not invent internal objectives, strategy, targets, or commissions.

## 3. Data feasibility

Establish, when available:

- grain;
- time coverage;
- relevant fields;
- keys and relationships where relevant;
- evident quality limitations;
- missing information; and
- necessary assumptions.

Separate proposed analysis into:

- **Supported**
- **Derivable**
- **Not supported**

Narrow infeasible ideas or identify the additional evidence required.

## 4. Connected analysis questions

Use three to five questions by default unless the project genuinely requires a different number.

Every question must contribute to the selected decision.

For each question state:

- the question;
- why it matters to the decision;
- required fields; and
- whether it is Supported or Derivable.

Move Not supported questions to the boundaries section and state what additional evidence would be required. Do not include them in the recommended core analysis.

Avoid disconnected exploratory questions added merely to make the project appear larger.

## 5. Measures or evaluation criteria

When measures are appropriate, use four to six core measures by default.

For each include:

- name;
- plain-language definition;
- decision relevance;
- calculation logic;
- required fields; and
- important caveat or interpretation risk.

Do not force KPI language onto projects better evaluated through tests, validation checks, model metrics, quality measures, or qualitative criteria.

Do not write DAX, SQL, Python, or spreadsheet formulas unless requested or the user has moved into the build stage.

## 6. Build and presentation plan

Outline the minimum useful work required for:

- preparation;
- modelling or structure where relevant;
- analysis;
- validation; and
- presentation.

Choose tools and final format according to the analytical goal rather than habit.

If visuals are useful, connect every recommended visual to a specific analytical question or decision.

Avoid decorative pages, duplicated metrics, or visuals that exist only because suitable columns are available.

## 7. Portfolio evidence

Specify what a reviewer should be able to inspect without needing the original working file.

Depending on the project, this may include:

- project brief and selected decision;
- data source, licensing, and limitations;
- cleaning and transformation decisions;
- modelling or methodological decisions;
- SQL queries, code, formulas, tests, or measure definitions;
- validation evidence;
- charts, report screenshots, notebook extracts, or process diagrams;
- findings and recommendations after analysis is completed; and
- concise repository or case-study documentation.

The portfolio should show reasoning and analytical decisions, not only the final output.

## 8. Boundaries and learner choices

End with:

- what is in scope;
- what is out of scope;
- what the data cannot support;
- the additional evidence required for any Not supported ideas;
- the small number of assumptions or trade-offs still requiring confirmation; and
- the next useful action.

Do not overwhelm the learner with unnecessary decisions.
